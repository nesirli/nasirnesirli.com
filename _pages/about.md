Most people who build genomics pipelines have never had to sign off a lab result. I spent over a decade in clinical laboratories, first as a clinical microbiologist and then managing laboratories across 26 regional sites, before I started building pipelines and models. So I know how clinical data is made, and where and why it goes wrong. That judgment shapes everything I build.

Today I build **clinical genomics pipelines**, from raw sequencing reads to benchmarked variant calls and finished clinical-style reports, and I apply machine learning to genomic and microbial data. I'm looking for **clinical bioinformatics roles in Ankara** in clinical genetics, molecular pathology or public-health microbiology.

## How I work

- **Benchmarked, not just run.** Every pipeline is measured against public truth sets (GIAB, SEQC2), with accuracy broken down by genomic context, the way clinical lab validation expects.
- **Reports are part of the pipeline.** Each repo generates a clinical-style PDF report, and every number in it comes from the pipeline's own output.
- **Built for the person running it.** QC gates catch real biological problems, outputs fit clinical reporting workflows, and documentation is written for the analyst as well as the developer.
- **Public or simulated data only.** No patient data, ever.
